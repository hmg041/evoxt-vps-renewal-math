## The clause that decides whether a discount is real

A discount you can't refund is a bet, not a saving. EDIS states plainly that orders using its location discount code are not refundable, allows one location change within 24 hours and only between the eight qualifying cities, and excludes the Interxion facility in Vienna. Get the location right before you pay.

TrumVPS offers a 7-day money-back guarantee, made subject to its refund policy, and its LET bonus of +50% RAM, +50% bandwidth or +50% SSD requires you to reply to the provider's forum thread with your order ID and pick one option per VPS. Vietnam orders get the selected bonus doubled. Nobody applies it for you, and it expires with the thread.

OrangeVPS had the most generous structure of the batch, a 50% discount that stayed on the account at every renewal, and it no longer matters because the purchase window shut on October 10. Its bonus required buying on specific dates and filing a ticket. Timing was the whole offer.

Hostinger gives 30 days to change your mind, and its FAQ says the discount doesn't change the service or configuration you receive. The refund window is the reason the 24-month prepay is survivable: you can test the box for a month and walk away with the $155.76 back if it doesn't perform.

Evoxt's pricing page doesn't publish a money-back window. Before you prepay three years to lock a rate, ask support in writing what the cancellation terms are for the term you're choosing, and keep the reply.

## Which plan for which job

512 MB at $2.99: a WireGuard endpoint, a DNS resolver, a jump host. Not a control panel, and 5 GB of disk won't hold much beyond the OS.

1 GB at $4.99: a static site behind nginx, a monitoring agent, a small bot. 10 GB is tight once Docker images start piling up.

2 GB at $5.99: one WordPress or Ghost install, or a single containerised app with a small database. This is the realistic floor for anything a client will look at.

4 GB at $11.99: several containers, Postgres alongside the app, a CI runner, a game server for a handful of players.

8 GB at $23.99: the point where self-hosted inference tools and heavier Java or Elixir services stop swapping. Choose VM-6 over VM-4 if the load is parallel rather than memory-hungry.

16 GB and up: database replicas, multi-tenant hosting, build farms. VM-12's 16 cores at $60.95 is the cheapest per-core point in the range, and it costs $12.96 more than VM-8 for eight additional cores.
